Reading the Monthly SEO Template as a Machine

Twenty-five tasks that run every month, plus seven more every third month. The sequence is right and the thinking behind it is better than most agency retainers ever get. The gap is between what the tasks say and what the system actually enforces, and closing it is mostly clicking.

What was reviewed

Every task description and its dependency list, fed in one at a time and mapped into a single graph. The verdict

The template works. One thing is wrong with it, and it repeats.

Every task states what it depends on in two separate places. There's the written Feeds line in the description, and there's the actual dependency setting in ClickUp that stops the task starting. The first is a sign on a door. The second is a lock.

In seven places there's a sign and no lock. The task can be ticked off before the work it depends on has happened, and nothing catches it.

Worst case is 1.8 Lock Monthly Plan. Its instructions say to review all seven Week 1 tasks. Four of them gate it. Every page task in Weeks 2 and 3 gets its target URL from this one.

Two other patterns worth naming, same root cause. Schema gets checked three times and page deletion gets checked zero times, because QA's scope is written as "2.1 to 3.2" and the pruning round is a quarterly task. And the rule for retiring dead tasks would delete your safety checks, because to that rule "found nothing" and "did nothing" look identical.

Most of the fix is clicking, not rework.

Fix before the next deploy

Seven findings where leaving it costs either a wrong decision that propagates through the month, or an irreversible action taken with nobody checking. Everything else can wait for the quarterly review.

011.8 Lock the plan

The keystone is wired to four of the seven tasks it says it reads

1.8 can be marked done while 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4 are still open.

Its first step is "review the deliverables of 1.1–1.7." Its second-to-last step is "confirm the 1.2 / 1.4 fix items are captured in 2.1, and that the 1.3 fix sheet is approved so 2.2 can start." So the written instructions require all seven. The dependency settings require only four: 1.1, 1.5, 1.6 and 1.7.

Lock the plan on Thursday, have the health sweep land Friday, and that confirm step was checking against nothing. Every page task in Weeks 2 and 3 gets its URL and refresh angle from this one, so a plan locked early carries into all of them.

FixWire 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4 as blockers on 1.8. Three clicks.
02Q2 Pruning round

The riskiest task in the system has the weakest safety rails

Pages get deleted with no named approver and no independent check.

It says "execute approved consolidations" and never says who approves. And 4.1's QA scope reads "every page changed this month, 2.1 to 3.2", which excludes quarterly tasks. So a title tag rewrite gets an independent check and a permanent 301 gets none.

The delete criteria are organic-only too. A page with no organic clicks can still take paid or email traffic and convert on it, which is why your own audit rules already exclude PPC landing pages from SEO metrics.

FixName the approver before the execute step, add client sign-off where content is deleted rather than merged, extend 4.1's QA scope to cover quarterly tasks, and check GA4 for non-organic sessions and conversions on every deletion candidate.
035.3 · Q6

The rule that keeps the template honest will delete the safety nets first

A check that finds nothing looks identical to a check that does nothing.

5.3 flags anything that "produced no value for the second straight month". Q6 restates it as "produced measurable deltas". Neither a clean health sweep, an unblocked crawler check, nor a QA pass where everything passes produces a delta, and all three are doing their job. 1.2 even says a stated "no issues" is a valid outcome, and Q6's wording would kill it anyway.

Run for a year, this keeps the tasks that always find something and removes the ones whose value is that they usually don't.

FixDefine value as output produced and consumed, never as problems found. Then copy the sentence the backlink defence check already uses, "log no action warranted with the export attached, the expected outcome most quarters", into 1.2, 1.4, 4.1 and 4.3. That protects them regardless of how the rule ends up worded.
045.2 → 5.3

Next month's entire cycle waits on a client attending a call

If the call slips two weeks, next month starts two weeks late.

The client call blocks month close, and month close both ends the month and creates next month's milestone. Monthly client calls get rescheduled constantly, for reasons nobody on your team controls. When one slips, the milestone isn't deployed, Week 1 doesn't start on time, and the following cycle runs late. This is the single biggest operational fragility in the template and it will show up within the first few months of live use.

FixGive 5.3 a timeout path. If the call hasn't happened by the last business day, deploy using the proposed direction from the report and adjust targets afterwards. Gate the confirmation of direction on the client, never the work itself.
05Q5 Entity review

The Wikidata instruction is wrong, and acting on it has a real downside

"Any real business qualifies, no notability bar" is not true.

Wikidata's notability policy requires serious, publicly available references. Plenty of businesses clear it, but an item sourced only to the client's own site and directory listings routinely gets nominated for deletion.

The cost isn't just wasted quarterly work. An account that keeps creating promotional items for paying clients attracts scrutiny, and a flagged account taints every client it has touched.

FixReplace with "verify serious, independent, publicly available references exist before creating an item; where they don't, log it as an entity gap". Name who holds the account and note the requirement to disclose the paid relationship.
063.3 Schema & entity

A task scoped to "every page changed this month" has no dependencies at all

It can run before the pages are changed, and nothing tells it otherwise.

The only fully disconnected node in the monthly graph, zero edges either direction. Its first step validates schema on every page changed this month, which is work owned by six other tasks. Nothing stops it running Monday and missing everything shipped Thursday.

It's also the third place schema gets validated, and the only one of the three not correctly sequenced. The template already knows the right shape here: keyword tracking is done in the refresh tasks and verified exactly once in QA. Schema didn't get the same treatment.

FixCut the schema step from 3.3 and let QA own schema verification, since it's already gated on all seven execution tasks. No coverage is lost. Keep the rest of the task, which is genuinely independent entity work.
071.4 → 1.6

The AI visibility reading can happen before anyone checks the crawlers can get in

A zero-visibility result isn't a null result if the CDN was blocking every AI bot.

The AI visibility run carries no blockers at all, so nothing stops it running before the crawler access check. The crawler check explicitly names it as a consumer, on the grounds that it "explains zero-visibility results if access is blocked". Run in the wrong order, you don't get a null reading, you get a wrong diagnosis, and it doesn't stay contained: it flows into target selection and becomes the mention outreach target list for the month.

FixWire 1.4 as a blocker on 1.6, or fold the robots and CDN check into the visibility run's first step.

Protect these

Nine things here are better than standard agency practice, and a few are the reason everything else works. Any edit should route around them.

Compile from task comments, not memory

The activity log. An explicit anti-fabrication control on the artifact most likely to get padded, and what makes "a task with no logged output is not done" enforceable.

Log "no action warranted", the expected outcome

The backlink defence check. Declares its own null result as success, which is the fix for finding 03 sitting in the template already.

The Never Include list

The monthly report. Naming what must not appear is rarer and more useful than naming what must, and three separate tasks defend the same line independently.

One team touches a page per month

The plan lock. Collision avoidance written as a rule rather than left to goodwill, with a clean boundary between optimisation scope and a real rewrite.

Never auto-roll incomplete work

Month close. Forces a decision on every unfinished item. Auto-rolling is how a backlog becomes a graveyard.

Roger approves every disavow entry

The backlink defence check. The right control on a high-stakes action, and the model the pruning round should copy.

Light monthly, deep quarterly

Crawler access with the quarterly crawl, and the entity spot-check with the citations audit. Cheap enough to actually happen, thorough enough to catch what the spot-check misses.

Every page passes or gets a fix task

QA. One of the few tasks that says what happens when the check fails instead of assuming it passes.

Refusing toxicity-score disavows

The backlink defence check. Correct, reasoned, and the opposite of what most agency templates do and bill for quarterly.

Cadence: move these

Worth adding Reviews are the one genuine hole in local coverage. The GBP check handles categories, services and hours monthly, the citations audit handles listing integrity quarterly, and review velocity, review response rate and the Q&A section appear in neither. The rest

Real, but none of it should hold up a deploy. Batch these into the first quarterly template review.

WhereFinding
2.5 → 4.3Title rewrites retarget up to five pages by front-loading query language, which is the intervention most likely to create query overlap, and it's the one page task excluded from the cannibalization check. All five come from the same candidate pool as the refresh targets, so they're topically adjacent by construction.
2.3 · 2.5The title spec says 60 characters and says nothing about the brand suffix. Your house standard is a 65-character budget for the page-specific portion with the suffix excluded, plus a no-repeated-token check. Two or more refreshes a month per client is enough volume for the two standards to visibly diverge.
4.4 · Q1 · Q4 · Q7Four tasks are never named by any other task. Their output gets consumed by pull rather than declared, which works, but it means the quarterly block is nearly invisible from inside the monthly cycle. Someone onboarded by reading Weeks 1 to 5 would finish not knowing the full crawl, citations audit and backlink defence check exist.
2.1 · 2.2The fix-list boundary is drawn by source tool, not by issue. Broken links appear in both a Search Console sweep and a SEMrush audit, so the same URL can be worked twice in one week by two different people.
Q3 → 1.6Revising the AI prompt set changes the denominator of share of voice, and neither the visibility run nor the report knows the basis moved. A quarterly step change gets read as a performance change. Handle it the way the Search Console impressions bug is already handled: stamp the version and caveat the revision month.
1.7Ownership conflict. The master rules assign it to Rahman directly, the task assigns it to Roger with Rahman conditional. Every other named task is consistent, so this one is the outlier.
Q1No severity tiers and no same-month escalation. A full quarterly crawl is the most likely task in the template to surface something urgent, and everything routes to next month regardless. Borrow the health sweep's Blocker / Fix this month / Watch vocabulary.
Q6Has a route to the master template but no step that goes looking for drift. Live example: 3.1 already carries a richer escape hatch for an unviable page than 2.3, the SOP it inherits from, and nothing prompts anyone to notice.
Q6The add and kill mechanisms sit in the same task but aren't linked, so the task count only grows. Killing means admitting something didn't work; adding doesn't. Fund each trial slot with a kill.
1.1Anomaly flagging has no thresholds, so it varies month to month and more once delegated. Give it numbers, and include a zero-fire check on conversion events, which is the failure that stays hidden longest.
1.1Rolling 30-day windows won't match a report the client reads as calendar months, and Search Console lags two to three days. Lock to complete calendar months and fix the pull date.
1.1 · 5.1Year-on-year impressions are unusable until the April 2027 report, because the prior-year period always falls inside the May 2025 to April 2026 logging-bug window. State that as a rule with an expiry date rather than leaving it to be re-derived monthly.
3.4"Top 1 to 2" has no selection criterion, and this is the only task converting AI visibility findings into action. At two per month, the ranking rule is the strategy. Carry-forwards also have no stale-out rule, so real capacity shrinks every month.
1.7 · 1.8 · Q3Three handoffs leave the template with no return path, and nothing ever asks whether a routed item shipped. Contrast the candidate list, which explicitly measures last month's keyword work before proposing new work.
SeveralFour load-bearing statistics carry no source. They justify their tasks existing and they travel to clients through the report. Record source and date beside each.
SeveralCross-month references are labelled inconsistently in five places, sometimes "next month's 1.8" and sometimes a bare "1.8". A bare backward reference reads as circular, which is worth avoiding in a document people audit.

One decision to make first

Are the Feeds lines documentation, or a specification? Right now they're treated as both, which is what produced most of these findings.

If they're authoritative, every Feeds edge should have a matching lock, and auditing the template becomes mechanical. A VA could run it in twenty minutes a quarter. If they're just prose for the reader, that's fine too, but then the wiring needs its own review and nobody should trust a Feeds line to say what gates what.

Second question, smaller, and it needs the one document I never saw. The monthly cycle assumes nine standing per-client artifacts exist and are current: the dashboard, the Site Health tracker, the share of voice tracker, the prompt set, the mentions tracker, Position Tracking with its month tags, last quarter's crawl archive, the topic gap refresh queue, and the vertical directory list. None of them names a location or an owner. That may be exactly what the standing Repeated Tasks parent covers. If it isn't, that's what breaks on client number four when the person who knew where everything lived is on holiday.
