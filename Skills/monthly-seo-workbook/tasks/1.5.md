## Windows: complete months, never rolling

The tab says 90 days versus the prior 90. Anchor both on **complete months**, so
running in August gives **May 1 - Jul 31** against **Feb 1 - Apr 30**: 92 and 89
days, no partial month. A rolling 90 days would end mid-August and pull half a
month of incomplete data into the comparison.

This does not conflict with 1.1's month-over-month window. 1.1 is a snapshot;
1.5 is trend detection, where one month is too noisy to call decay. Different
jobs, different windows, each stated on its own tab.

### Year-over-year control is MANDATORY - owner-caught gap, 2026-08-19

Sequential windows alone mis-grade seasonal content in BOTH directions, and
EW proved both on the same day:

- **Under-flagging:** the 30-60-90 new-principal guide read as "light refresh,
  protect what works" sequentially (-46%), but its peak season IS summer -
  new principals prep before the school year - and the same-window YoY read
  was **-86%** (929 clicks summer 2025 vs 133 summer 2026). The sequential
  view hid a collapse. What-to-look-for: sequential -49%, YoY **-89%**.
- **Over-flagging risk:** T-TESS content craters every June (Texas summer)
  and surges from mid-July; a sequential drop into summer on school-year
  content is calendar, not decay.

So every candidate row's decay verdict must carry the **same-window
year-over-year comparison** alongside the sequential one, and severity is
graded off the YoY number wherever a prior year exists. A page with no prior
-year data gets judged against its own topic-family's current trend (the
how-do-t-tess-impact-teacher-ratings call: -68% sequential while its T-TESS
siblings surged = real decline, not summer).

## Section A is empty on a client's first run

It wants last month's optimizations from Position Tracking's previous tag. On
the first run there is no such record, because what we changed is a record of
our own work and is not recoverable from ranking data. Write the baseline note
and move on. From the following month it fills from whatever 1.8 selected.

**Never back-fill it from ranking history.** A position change with no record of
a change made is not a before/after.

## Three data traps, all found the hard way on EW

### 1. GSC reports anchor links as separate pages

154 of EW's 1,227 page rows were Elementor table-of-contents anchors
(`#elementor-toc__heading-anchor-0`, `#2`, `#5`), carrying **8,812 impressions
and one click between them**. The Alabama guide appeared seven times: once as
itself and six times as anchors.

Unmerged, they split a page's impressions across phantom rows so no row shows
true volume, and every anchor row is a guaranteed false CTR anomaly by
construction. `norm()` strips the fragment and `merge()` sums onto the canonical
URL, re-deriving position weighted by impressions.

### 2. Never retype a URL

Two candidates reached the sheet with wrong URLs because they were retyped from
truncated console output: the real page was
`...-alabama-teacher-observation-tool-atot/`, and a near-duplicate stub existed
alongside the real classroom-walkthroughs page. Carry URLs from the data.

### 3. Use the client's own CTR curve, never a generic one

EW earns **2.05%** at positions 4-5, **0.90%** at 6-7, **0.52%** at 8-9, roughly
a third of typical curves. Two pages looked like CTR failures against a generic
curve and were exactly average for this site. One page at 4.30% is the best
converter on the site and its note says explicitly **not** to touch the snippet.

Measured against the wrong baseline, the advice inverts.

## Diagnose the cause before proposing a fix

Clicks falling is a symptom with two opposite causes, and the fix differs:

| Evidence | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Position moved 1.5+ places worse | Ranking loss | content depth and links |
| Position held, impressions fell | Demand or seasonality | check same months last year; do not rewrite |
| Position and impressions held | CTR gap | title and meta, judged against the site's own curve |

Pull twelve-month history for every selected candidate. On EW this reversed the
headline recommendation: `/t-tess-rubric` looked like the top target on
click-loss size, and its position had not moved in a year while its impressions
tracked Texas evaluation season.

**Seasonal clients need this most.** May-Jul against Feb-Apr crosses a season, so
on the HVAC clients heating queries will "decay" 60-80% because it is July.
Where position held and impressions fell, say so and require a year-over-year
check before anyone actions it.

## Pool decay AND striking distance, or four clients get the wrong tab

Ranking on click loss alone fills the home-services tabs with DIY blog posts:
how to unclog a garbage disposal, how to thaw frozen pipes, why more people do
not install home urinals. Meanwhile the money sits in striking distance on
service pages against local commercial queries, and none of it appears:

- Superior `/hvac/furnace-repair` position 7.1, "furnace repair salt lake city"
- Beehive `/emergency-plumber` position 8.5, "emergency plumber salt lake city"
- Comfort `/service-areas/sandy-utah` position 11.9, "furnace repair sandy"
- SameDay `/plumbing` positions 10.6-14.4, "plumber near me"

Build ONE candidate pool from both sources, plus CTR anomalies, then rank it.
Decay measures what was lost; striking distance measures what is within reach.
A tab that only shows the first is a report on the past.

## Business value, and what it decides

**A money page is High. Full stop.** The client designated it as a page that
sells, and no keyword test improves on that. A buy-intent word list graded
Snow's product pages Medium because "purple teeth whitening strips" contains no
word from the list, when someone searching a product by name is as close to
buying as a person gets.

**Only posts need an intent judgement**, because a post can serve a buyer (cost,
comparison, "near me") or a DIY reader who will never convert. Commercial-intent
post is Medium; informational is Low.

**Rank by value band before score.** Beehive and Cudio both put a Low
informational post at rank 1 on raw impressions alone. A row marked N must never
head the list.

**The homepage is always a money page.** `norm()` strips the trailing slash, so
it stops matching the money set unless handled explicitly; Beehive's homepage
was dropped from candidacy entirely while carrying 100,856 impressions and a
fall from position 20.8 to 24.2.

**Every High is selected.** If a High page is not selected, the reason must be
about its value, not about which diagnosis was reached. "No defect found" is a
reason the ACTION differs, not a reason to exclude: EW's largest asset is
selected with the action "validate seasonality, then snippet work" rather than
a rewrite it does not need.

**Every row gets Y or N. No blanks.** A blank is an undecided cell in a document
whose whole purpose is decisions.

Rank by recoverable opportunity so 1.8 takes the top 3-5 in order.

## Store the client's own CTR curve in Section A

Every tab records its client's measured CTR by position band for the window.
Next month's CTR claims get judged against that, not a generic curve. On EW this
reversed the headline recommendation: its 0.90% at positions 6-7 made a page
that looked broken merely average, and a page at 4.30% turned out to be the best
converter on the site with a note not to touch its snippet.
