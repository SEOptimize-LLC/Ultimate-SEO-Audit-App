## Two signals per bot, and they are not equivalent

**robots.txt** is authoritative, complete and free. It is parsed properly here
rather than string-matched: consecutive `User-agent` lines share one rule block,
the most specific matching group wins, and `*` applies **only** when no named
group matches. A named group beats `*` even when it is empty, which is how a
site ends up granting one bot blanket access while restricting everything else.
The sheet always names which group and which directive decided the verdict, so
nobody has to re-read the file to check the answer.

**A live fetch** with the bot's own user-agent string is the second signal, and
its two outcomes carry very different weight:

- **403 / 401 / 429 is conclusive.** Something is refusing that bot.
- **200 is NOT proof of access.** Cloudflare verifies AI bots by IP and
  signature, not by the user-agent header, so a spoofed UA from our address can
  sail straight through while the real bot is blocked at the edge. Six of the
  eight clients sit behind Cloudflare.

Every row on a Cloudflare client carries that caveat in its note. A green result
that overstates itself is worse than an amber one that explains itself, and this
is exactly the trap that produced the stale-noindex mistake in 1.2.

## Training crawlers are flagged, never fixed

GPTBot, CCBot, Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended feed model **training**.
Allowing them puts the client's content into training data, which is a
commercial decision belonging to the client, not an SEO defect to correct.

Blocking them does **not** affect whether the site appears in AI search answers.
That is governed by the search bots (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot,
PerplexityBot) and the user-triggered fetchers. Conflating the two is the most
common error in this area: a client who blocks GPTBot has not blocked ChatGPT
from citing them.

The row writes `Flagged`, states the current position for each, and says raise
it with the client. Never change it unilaterally, in either direction.

## Section B: what a crawler receives with JavaScript switched off

**The homepage every time, plus up to 5 URLs per template.** Roughly 11 to 38
URLs per client regardless of size. Each is fetched with an AI bot user-agent
and measured for word count, H1, H2 and internal links.

### Why per template and not a percentage

What section B tests is a property of the **template**, not the page. Pages of
one template come out of one code path, so a client-rendered template is caught
by the first sample and the fortieth adds nothing. Arsenal reads identically at
3 URLs or 300, because every route serves a byte-identical shell.

A percentage also misallocates. Snow is ~970 blog posts out of 1,009 in-scope
URLs, so 2% would spend 18 of its 20 requests on posts and barely touch products
and collections, which are the commercially important templates. Five per
template spends the same budget across all three.

And for the rare defect a percentage does not help anyway: if 1% of pages hide
their content behind a JS widget, 20 random samples find one about a fifth of
the time. Sampling is good at common problems and bad at rare ones, and the
common problem is the one a single sample catches.

### Grouping

Posts are identified from the **sitemap group** they came from, never from the
URL path. Superior's posts sit at the root (`/why-is-my-toilet-running/`) and
its rule is the sitemap name `post-sitemap`, so path matching filed every post
as its own template and produced 192 "templates". That is the third time a
sitemap-name rule has been mistaken for a URL-substring rule in this project;
`crawl_targets.collect` already knows the difference, so classify with it.

Everything else groups on its top path folder. Root-level pages are pooled into
one "money page (root level)" group, because a flat architecture otherwise files
each page as its own template: Beehive produced 55 and Arsenal 30, which is just
a page list wearing a different hat.

The sample is taken **evenly spaced across the sorted group**, not the first
five, which on a city-page set would otherwise return five neighbouring towns.

### Only pages with SEO value - owner rule, 2026-08-19

The render sample checks **content the client is trying to rank**, never
legal/utility/sales-collateral templates. Per-client exclusions live in
`crawl.access_exclude` in the registry (EW: `/case-studies/`,
`/instructional-look-fors/`, `/pricing`, `/terms/`) and are applied AFTER
grouping, so they cover the sitemap path AND the GSC scope fallback - the
fallback is exactly where EW's terms and case-study rows slipped in on the
first run. `access_exclude` deliberately does NOT touch `money_pages`: pricing
and case studies keep their money-page status for the tasks that legitimately
use it (conversion paths); this list narrows 1.4's sample alone.

When a locked monthly plan exists (`monthly_plan/plan.yaml`), its 1.8 targets
are **seeded into the sample by name** with the label `1.8 target` - the pages
this month's work depends on get checked deliberately, not left to the luck of
even spacing.

### A refusal message must match its control - bug fixed 2026-08-19

Two flags, two claims: `reachable` (our address gets a 200 with a plain
browser UA) decides whether a bot 403 is ATTRIBUTABLE; `measurable` (any
route, DFS included, returns the page) decides whether section B can be
graded. The WAF-row message once claimed "an ordinary browser user-agent was
served normally" whenever the site was merely *measurable* - on EW that
asserted a control result that never happened, while our own IP was being
refused across the board. The message now states, in the blocked case, that
the bot 403s are NOT attributable and live fetches need a re-test from a
different address. Section B notes also say when a page was fetched via
DataForSEO instead of directly.

### Rendered comparison, only on failures

A page that fails the raw check is then rendered in a real browser, so the sheet
carries the delta rather than a bare FAIL. Costs nothing on the seven clients
whose raw HTML is already complete.

Arsenal's `/hormone-therapy` is the case that justifies it: **8 words raw
against 1,810 rendered**, 0 headings against 45, no JSON-LD against 1 block, and
a title of "Arsenal Health | Transform Your Health & Performance" raw against
"Hormone Therapy Denver | Arsenal Health" rendered. The raw version cannot even
be identified as being about hormone therapy. Googlebot renders and indexes it
fine; OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot largely do not execute
JS, which is why robots.txt access and readability are separate rows.

### Section B needs rows made for it

The tab ships with four rows between the B heading and section C. A real sample
does not fit, so `sheets_io.insert_rows` adds exactly what is needed and the
anchors are re-read afterwards, because inserting rows moves them.

Pass needs **120+ words and at least one H1**. Below that, the page has nothing
an assistant could quote.

**Arsenal fails this outright**: 8 words, 0 H1, 0 H2 on both its homepage and
its money page, because the site is a client-rendered SPA. Its robots.txt is the
only one of the eight explicitly configured to welcome AI bots, so someone did
the easy half and the site still cannot be read. Access and readability are
separate problems and a pass on A says nothing about B.

## When the site refuses us: the fallback chain

Some clients sit behind a WAF that rejects datacenter addresses. Education
Walkthrough returns 403 to this machine on every user-agent including plain
Chrome, and to WebFetch as well. **DataForSEO fetches from its own addresses and
gets through**, so three separate fallbacks now fire automatically:

| What fails | Fallback | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Page HTML | `dfs_page()` via DFS instant_pages, JS off | Otherwise section B reports a Fail that measures our network |
| `robots.txt` | `dfs_robots()` | See below. This is the dangerous one. |
| Sitemap | `scope_fallback()` from `Clients/<folder>/url_scope.txt`, built from GSC pages | Otherwise section B silently shrinks to the homepage alone |

**The robots.txt failure is the one to fear, because it fails silently in the
safe-looking direction.** A 403 yields an empty rule set, an empty rule set means
"no rule for this bot", and "no rule" means allowed. Section A then reports every
AI crawler as **Pass** without a single byte of the file having been read. EW
shipped exactly that, and the verdicts happened to be right, which is worse: the
same output would have appeared if the file blocked every crawler alive.

DFS returns robots.txt as parsed allow/disallow lists, so **user-agent grouping is
lost** and everything reconstructs under `*`. That proves no blanket block
exists. It cannot prove a named bot is unblocked, and the caveat goes in the
sheet.

The GSC scope file only covers URLs that have had impressions, so a page that has
never ranked is invisible to it. Also stated wherever it is used.

## `reachable` and `measurable` are different questions

Keep these apart. Conflating them wrote four false "Unblock OAI-SearchBot" style
fixes into a live sheet against a site whose robots.txt allows every one of them.

- **`reachable`** — can *we* reach it from this address. Governs whether a live
  403 is **attributable**. The bot probes leave from this machine, so if plain
  Chrome is refused here, a bot 403 here is evidence about our IP and nothing
  else. **Never satisfy this with a proxy fallback.**
- **`measurable`** — is it reachable by any route at all. Governs whether section
  B can run. The DFS fallback satisfies this legitimately.

## Two different defects fail section B

Threshold is 120+ words and at least one H1, but a miss can mean either thing and
they route to opposite fixes:

- **Thin raw HTML** (`words < 120`) is a rendering problem. Fix: server-render or
  pre-render. Arsenal's 8-words-raw against 1,810-rendered is the case.
- **Content present, no H1** is not. EW's `/case-studies/paragon-case-study/` has
  983 words and 18 H2s with 0 H1. It was reported as "an AI crawler sees
  effectively nothing here" and routed to server-rendering, which would have sent
  a developer to rebuild a page that renders perfectly. Fix: add an H1, and the
  row says explicitly not to route it to rendering work.
